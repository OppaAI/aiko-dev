🤖 × 🪰🧠 AI x Fruit Fly Brain Part 2

🧠🤖 Aiko is now connected to an entire fruit-fly brain connectome.
I’ve been experimenting with something a little unusual: taking Aiko, my local AI companion/agent, and connecting parts of her cognitive architecture to the MaleCNS v1.0 fruit-fly connectome.
And the latest update is a big step forward.
Aiko now has access to a 166,606-neuron / 25.6-million-connection representation of the MaleCNS brain.

The result is a hybrid architecture:
🪰 Mushroom Body
→ real connectome-derived neurons and synapses
→ associative memory
→ learned approach/avoidance
→ dopamine/reinforcement-based plasticity
→ persistent learned changes

🧭 Central Complex
→ real connectome-derived circuitry
→ focus/heading
→ decisiveness
→ sleep pressure

👁️ Fly sensory circuits
→ antennal-lobe-inspired sensory normalization
→ T4/T5 motion processing
→ sensory gain and motion salience

🧠 Whole MaleCNS graph
→ 166,606 neurons
→ 25.6M directed connections
→ two-hop rate propagation
→ descending-neuron, clock and arousal readouts

Then all of those signals feed into Aiko’s existing AI architecture through a shared NeuralState.
That means the fly-derived circuitry can influence things such as:
• what she remembers
• what she approaches or avoids
• where her attention goes
• how urgent something feels
• how energetic or tired she is
• her circadian state
• how strongly she speaks
• her expression and body behavior
• how cautiously she approaches certain external actions

So instead of:
LLM → response
the architecture is becoming more like:
senses → biological circuits → neural state → memory / reasoning / agent → voice / body
And now I can actually watch part of that process happening in the Fly Brain Neuralnet Studio.
The visualization in this video is showing a sampled/procedural view of the much larger connectome, together with Aiko’s live neural-state and output signals.

There is still a lot left to do.
The current whole-brain layer is rate-based rather than spiking, the dynamics are partly functional abstractions, and many biological circuits are still represented as scaffolds rather than complete biological simulations.

But this is the direction I wanted to explore:
What happens when an AI agent doesn’t just use an LLM, memory and tools, but also has a small piece of real biological network architecture influencing how it processes experience?
